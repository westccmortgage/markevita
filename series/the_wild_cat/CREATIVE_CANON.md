# The Wild Cat — Creative Canon and Story Arc

Status: canonical creative direction for future episodes.  
Episode 1 currently in production is not to be changed by this document.

## Core character rule — The Wild Cat

The Wild Cat is a fully realistic, anatomically natural large female wild forest cat. She is never anthropomorphic in body design and is never literally shown as a human woman.

Her feminine identity is communicated gradually through performance rather than human anatomy. The audience should see a real wild cat and increasingly feel the personality of a woman within her.

Use:
- unusually expressive almond-shaped eyes and intelligent sustained eye contact;
- subtle feline half-smirks created only by eyes, head angle, ears, whiskers and natural mouth posture;
- graceful, deliberate movement and proud posture;
- playful defiance, curiosity, tenderness, jealousy, hurt pride and selective affection;
- moments where she deliberately looks away after being noticed, then checks whether the Hunter is still watching;
- increasing confidence around the Hunter without becoming domesticated.

Never use:
- human eyelashes, makeup, lipstick, human lips, human hair or hairstyle;
- breasts, clothing, jewelry, human hands, human facial structure or bipedal behavior;
- exaggerated cartoon expressions;
- spoken human dialogue from the Wild Cat.

The emotional principle is: **the viewer sees a wild animal, but intuitively reads a woman.**

## Progression across the first arc

Episode 1 — **The First Gift**  
She is mostly animal instinct: hungry, suspicious, exhausted and alone. Femininity is only a trace in the eyes and the final choice to remain near the fire. Do not retroactively alter the Episode 1 production while it is running.

Episode 2 — **The Shadow**  
Her personality begins to emerge. She follows the Hunter while pretending not to follow him. She studies him. When he turns toward her, she sometimes looks away with almost theatrical indifference. She begins to test how close she can come without surrendering her freedom. The first playful half-smirk appears. The Hunter starts to understand that she is not merely following food; she is choosing his company.

Episode 3 — **Too Close**  
Affection becomes harder to hide. She shows pride, possessiveness and the first hint of jealousy when another animal receives the Hunter's attention. She immediately retreats when he notices. Their bond now resembles courtship emotionally, while remaining completely natural in animal behavior.

Episode 4 — **The Wound**  
The Hunter says or does something that she interprets as rejection or control. Her response is unmistakably personal: hurt pride before anger. She leaves rather than submit.

Episode 5 — **The Edge of the World**  
At the ocean she has complete freedom, but discovers that freedom without the person she chose feels empty. Minimal narration. Her eyes and behavior carry the episode.

Episode 6 — **She Came Back**  
The reunion. No sentimental speech. The Hunter does not capture or command her. She approaches him by choice. Her forehead touches his hand. This is the emotional confirmation that neither owns the other.

Episode 7 — **Smoke**  
Their established partnership is interrupted when she detects danger before he does. The wildfire begins.

Episode 8 — **Fire**  
They survive and help each other through the burning forest. Their relationship is proven through action rather than dialogue.

Episode 9 — **After the Fire**  
Among the first green shoots after the burn, the Hunter finally understands the nature of their bond: he never tamed her. She stayed.

## Episode 2 — production story direction

Target: 120 seconds, 9:16, English, fairy-tale drama/adventure.  
No burned-in captions or on-screen text. Sparse English narrator voice-over only.

Opening: dawn after the first night by the fire. The Hunter wakes and the Wild Cat appears to be gone.

Middle: he walks through the forest. We repeatedly discover that she is following him at a distance — behind a fallen trunk, across a stream, on a mossy ridge. Each time he notices her, she acts as if she was never following him. Her behavior becomes subtly playful. He stops leaving food as bait and simply continues walking, giving her the choice.

Key emotional beat: at a stream, the Hunter sits and drinks. The Wild Cat approaches closer than ever. He looks directly at her. She holds his gaze, gives the faintest natural feline half-smirk, then deliberately turns her head away. He almost smiles.

Final sequence: evening. The Hunter reaches camp and lights the fire without looking behind him. After a pause, the Wild Cat walks into the firelight on her own and settles slightly closer than the previous night.

Narrator, final line: **“She still belonged to the forest. But now, every path she chose seemed to find him.”**

Cliffhanger into Episode 3: the next morning the Hunter wakes to find a freshly caught rabbit placed beside his pack. The Wild Cat watches from a distance. For the first time, she has brought *him* a gift.

## Direction rule

Do not make the feminine quality obvious all at once. It should emerge episode by episode as trust grows. Episode 2 is the first time the audience should consciously wonder whether there is something almost human in the way she looks at him — but the camera must never prove or explain it.
